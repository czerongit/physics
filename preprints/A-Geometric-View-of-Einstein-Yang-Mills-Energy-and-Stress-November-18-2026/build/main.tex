\begin{abstract}
Yang--Mills theory describes interacting gauge fields whose classical Lagrangian contains no gluon mass term. A topological view describes how the radial and transverse directions of a field define oriented phase planes, each carrying a U(1) rotation. Coupling the field and its successive responses links three such planes, generating an SU(3) symmetry whose eight components are represented by the faces of an octahedron.

The resulting closed structure separates its interior from the surrounding spacetime. Its coupled responses determine a spacetime metric and curvature. Variation of the curvature energy yields the stress--energy tensor, including the gravitational response surrounding the structure. Its energy and momentum determine its mass without introducing an elementary gluon mass term into the Yang--Mills Lagrangian. Subtracting the vacuum energy from the sum of the interior and exterior energies relates the mass of the closed field to the lower edge of its excitation spectrum.
\end{abstract}
